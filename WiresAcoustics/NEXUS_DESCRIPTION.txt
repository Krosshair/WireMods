[size=6][b]Wire's Acoustics[/b][/size]

Sound physics for Valheim. The world around you shapes every sound: rooms ring, walls muffle, cliffs echo, and far-off sounds arrive late. Client side.

Everything is heard from the camera, never from the character.

[size=5][b]How it works[/b][/size]

The mod traces rays through the world, the way light is traced in a renderer, but for sound. A few times a second, rays go out from the camera in every direction and find out what's around you: how closed in you are, how big the space is, what it's made of, and where the openings are. Every sound you hear is then shaped by that space and by everything between it and you.


[size=5][b]Features[/b][/size]

[size=4][b]Reverb from the real space[/b][/size]
The reverb isn't a preset. It's worked out from what the rays hit, so a stone hall rings, a log cabin sounds warm, a cave sounds like a cave, and a furnished room sounds lived in while an empty one echoes. Outdoors is measured the same way: open ground is dry, and a forest has its own soft tail from the trunks around you. The game's fixed reverb is replaced everywhere.

[size=4][b]Muffling through walls[/b][/size]
Walls, floors, hills and rocks between you and a sound make it quieter and duller, more so through several layers. Furniture and trees muffle a little. The thing making the sound never hides itself.

[size=4][b]Sound finds its way round[/b][/size]
A sound behind a wall also reaches you through the openings: a window, a door, a hole in the roof, round a corner, or bouncing off a surface. You hear it from where it comes through, so a deer outside is heard at the window, not through the wall.

[size=4][b]Echo[/b][/size]
Out in the open, a cliff or a wall facing you sends sounds back from its side, a moment later. Bare rock echoes clearly; a snowy slope barely does.

[size=4][b]The ground matters[/b][/size]
Snow swallows sound, grass and forest floors soften it, stone and paving reflect it, and water reflects almost everything.

[size=4][b]Sound takes time[/b][/size]
Sound travels at the speed of sound. A tree falling far away is heard after you see it, and a distant sound stops that much later too.

[size=4][b]Doppler[/b][/size]
Moving sounds rise in pitch as they come toward you and drop as they go away.

[size=4][b]Distance[/b][/size]
Sounds fade with distance the way real sound does, instead of cutting out after a few metres. Quiet sounds fade sooner than loud ones (a sconce's crackle is gone long before a hearth's roar), the air takes the highs off far sounds, and the wind carries sound downwind.

[size=4][b]HRTF (headphones)[/b][/size]
HRTF stands for head-related transfer function: how your head and ears change a sound depending on where it comes from. A sound on your left reaches your right ear a tiny bit later and duller, sounds behind you lose some brightness, and your outer ear colours sounds from above and below. The mod models this itself, so you can tell front from back and up from down, not just left from right. Best with headphones.

[size=4][b]Sounds have a place and a shape[/b][/size]
Creature sounds follow the creature, a death wail follows the falling body, and a creature's calls are duller when it faces away. Big sources (a hearth, a cart, a troll) sound as wide as they look and are only hidden when all of them is. Your own sounds (steps, equipping, drawing a bow) come from your character, even the ones the game plays "in the camera".

[size=4][b]Weather and the sea[/b][/size]
Rain and wind indoors come muffled through the walls and clearer from the window. The sea is heard from the actual shore. Under water, everything is muffled.

[size=4][b]Portals[/b][/size]
Going through a portal, its hum doesn't stop and restart: it carries through with you and keeps playing from the exit portal.

[size=5][b]Performance[/b][/size]

The ray tracing runs on Unity's worker threads, the per-sound processing on the audio thread, and the main thread only does a small amount of bookkeeping: about 0.1 ms per frame. Sounds have levels of detail like a game's models: the nearest get the full HRTF, the rest a lighter version you can't tell apart at that distance, and the count adjusts to how busy the audio thread actually is.
