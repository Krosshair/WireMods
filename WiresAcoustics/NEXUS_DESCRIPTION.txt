[size=5][b]How it works[/b][/size]

The mod traces rays through the world, the way light is traced in a renderer, but for sound. A few times a second, rays go out from your character's head in every direction and find out what's around you: how closed in you are, how big the space is, what it's made of, and where the openings are. Every sound you hear is then shaped by that space and by everything between it and you.

Which way a sound is comes from the camera, as you see it. How far it is, what's in the way and which room you're in come from your character's head, so zooming out doesn't make your own steps quieter and the camera slipping behind a wall doesn't muffle the world.

Wherever I could, the numbers come from real measurements and standards instead of being made up. The sources are at the bottom.

[size=5][b]Features[/b][/size]

[size=4][b]Reverb from the real space[/b][/size]
The reverb is worked out from what the rays hit: how big the space is, how closed in, and what it's made of. A stone hall rings, a log cabin sounds warmer, a cave sounds like a cave, and a furnished room is drier than an empty one. Windows, doors and gaps let sound out instead of counting the sky behind them as part of the room. Open ground is dry.

[size=4][b]Walls block sound like real walls[/b][/size]
Walls, floors, roofs and doors block sound differently by what they're made of. A door lets through more than a plank wall, and stone keeps out more than wood. The next room is muffled, not silent. As you walk behind the edge of a wall, the sound gradually loses what's in the open and comes through or round the wall instead. Being just behind an edge isn't the same as standing in the middle of its shadow.

[size=4][b]Sound goes round things[/b][/size]
A sound behind a wall also reaches you through openings: a window, a door, a hole in the roof, or round a corner. More than one opening can carry the same sound, and as you walk the direction shifts rather than hopping from window to window. The sound through the wall arrives first and helps set its direction; a much louder arrival through an opening can take over, as hearing does in real life. Hills, rocks, trees and furniture in the way muffle by their size: a trunk barely matters, a boulder takes the highs off, a hill cuts a sound depending on how high it rises.

[size=4][b]Material matters[/b][/size]
Every surface absorbs the lows and highs by published data for what it is. Snow swallows sound, moss and forest floors soften it, grass a bit less, paving and stone reflect it, water reflects almost everything. Just like IRL. Cool, right?

[size=4][b]Distance and air[/b][/size]
Sounds fade with distance the way real sound does, instead of cutting out after a few metres: a quiet sound still reaches far, just quietly. Sound takes time to travel, so distant sounds arrive late; sounds coming towards you or moving away shift in pitch. A small fire is quieter than a hearth. The air takes the highs off far sounds as the international standard says it does, with the weather: warm, cold and freezing are matched to the real Norwegian weather Valheim is modelled on.

[size=4][b]Wind[/b][/size]
Downwind, distant sounds carry further; upwind, they can get quieter. How much depends on the wind, distance and ground, and it drops away when you're sheltered behind a wall, rock or hill. The wind itself is shaped by what's round you: in the lee of something it drops to a low rumble, it's heard from where it comes round and over what's in the way, and it gusts and lulls instead of blowing as one even loop. The reeds in the Plains follow the wind too, without making birds and rain come from one direction.

[size=4][b]HRTF (headphones)[/b][/size]
HRTF stands for head-related transfer function: how your head and ears change a sound depending on where it comes from. A sound on your left reaches your right ear a tiny bit later and duller, sounds behind you lose some brightness, and your outer ear colours sounds from above and below. The mod models this itself, for every sound, so you can tell front from back and up from down, not just left from right. Best with headphones. [i]I'd recommend turning HRTF off on speakers, as your real head already produces ACTUAL HRTF.[/i] For the mod's own HRTF to sound its best, turn off Windows Sonic or Dolby Atmos spatial processing on your output device while playing Valheim; running both spatializers together can reduce directional clarity.

[size=4][b]Sounds come from where they're made[/b][/size]
The game plays a lot of sounds at a character's feet or at the base of a piece. Here, weapon swings come from the moving weapon, a bow's draw from the bow, holstering from your back, eating from your head. Creatures roar from their head, claw with their arm and stomp with their feet. Chests open at the lid, doors sound from their hinges, fires and furnaces from the flame, crafting from the station, wards and traders from their heads. Whatever you're using, building, removing or repairing is heard clearly while it's within your reach, even in a tight wall of chests. Raising the terrain sounds from the new ground surface, not from underneath it. Rotated things keep their sounds in the right place, and big sources sound as wide as they look. A specific hit or action still comes from where it actually happened.

[size=4][b]Weather and the sea[/b][/size]
Rain, wind and birds sound like they're outside, not in the room with you. Windows, doors and gaps let more of them in, while walls let through a quieter, duller sound. The rain stays audible indoors, and an actual roof gives the original Valheim recording a little more presence. A floor between storeys doesn't count as a roof. The sea comes from the real shoreline around you: a house blocks the part behind it without silencing the whole coast. Under water, the world above is about 30 dB quieter, as the physics of the water surface says.

[size=4][b]Portals[/b][/size]
Going through a portal, its hum doesn't stop and restart: it carries through with you and keeps playing from the exit portal. More of an extra than sound physics, but it's audio-related so it's here.

[size=5][b]Performance[/b][/size]

The ray tracing runs on Unity's worker threads, and the per-sound processing runs on the audio thread: sounds too quiet to hear aren't processed. The mod asks Unity to mix 128 sounds at once instead of the game's 32, so in a busy base far sounds don't drop out and nothing is cut off mid-sound. The game's audio update does a small amount of bookkeeping on the main thread. Paths round walls are only traced for sounds that are blocked, and rooms that don't change are scanned less often, with a refresh when something is built, removed or a door opens.

Every value below was checked against the source it's credited to.

Iron and ice: the mass law, as in [url=https://web.mit.edu/parmstr/Public/NRCan/CanBldgDigests/cbd239_e.html]Warnock, Factors Affecting Sound Transmission Loss[/url] (NRC Canadian Building Digest 239, 1985).
[*][b]Surface absorption:[/b] [url=https://sengpielaudio.com/calculator-RT60Coeff.htm]Sengpiel's table[/url] (brick, marble, wood on joists, water or ice, concrete); the [url=https://pyroomacoustics.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyroomacoustics.materials.database.html]pyroomacoustics materials database[/url] (furniture, rugs, curtains, limestone); [url=https://heyizhou.net/notes/absorption-coefficients]heyizhou.net's absorption tables[/url] (steel decking; heavy wood from Vorländer's Auralization).
[*][b]Ground:[/b] the Nord2000 ground classes (flow resistivity), as listed in the [url=https://www.dbaudio.com/assets/products/downloads/software/noizcalc/dbaudio-noizcalc-technical-white-paper-1.6-en.pdf]d&b NoizCalc technical white paper[/url]; turned into absorption with Delany and Bazley's model ([url=https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-682X(70)90031-9]Acoustical properties of fibrous absorbent materials[/url], Applied Acoustics 3, 1970).

[*][b]Room size and reverb:[/b] Sabine's reverberation formula, RT60 = 0.161 V / A ([url=https://sengpielaudio.com/calculator-RT60.htm]Sengpiel[/url]); the critical distance, 0.057 √(QV/RT) ([url=https://strutt.arup.com/help/Electroacoustics/DCritical.htm]Arup[/url]).
[*][b]Wind:[/b] [url=https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/dc_waddington_yw_lam_the_effect_of_weather_conditions_on_the_propagation_of_sound.pdf]Waddington and Lam, The effect of weather conditions on the propagation of sound[/url] (Proceedings of the IOA 24, 2002); the US National Weather Service's [url=https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=bliz]blizzard definition[/url] to match the game's wind to real wind speeds.

Not from a source (judgement values): the wind in the lee of things (12 dB less, down to 800 Hz) and its gusts (up to 4 dB either way), how partial source coverage and several walls are blended, a wall counting once however many pieces it's built from, indoors 3 dB softer first reflections and 4 dB darker reverb (cozy), random calls kept off buildings within 10 m of you (moved out 4 m at a time), where you count as inside (a third to two thirds closed in), the reverb's loudness and tone, rooms heard at half their built size and as furnished homes (Valheim builds are about twice real size and bare), how much soft obstacles muffle, how much trees enclose a space, props' absorption, random bird and insect calls sitting 1 m above whatever is at their spot, the limits applied to wind propagation outside the measured range, the HRTF's amounts (level, shading, the outer ear's notch), past the game's own range for a sound, 6 dB more each time the distance doubles (instead of a cut-off), fires' relative loudness based on the original sound range, where round a wall the mod looks for a way (just past the edge of what blocks it, 1 to 7 m to the sides and up to 5.5 m over), when something counts as in the way (0.3 of a wall), a player's own unplaced sounds made placed with a near distance of at least 1 m, the wind in your ears (up to 2 dB louder facing it, 3 dB quieter with your back to it), the two rays beside every line 8 cm off (two of three must agree), a line through more than 16 things counting as through a plank wall at least, and a door's sounds 15 cm in from its hinge.
